From Artificial Intelligence to Machine Learning

A rigorous, progressive curriculum for a computer-science graduate — built on the principle of understanding why before how. We travel from the definition of AI to implementing core ML algorithms from scratch, and we deliberately stop at the edge of Deep Learning.

29 lessons 8 modules 9 live · new lessons in progress Scope: AI landscape → Classical ML Tools: NumPy · Pandas · Matplotlib · Scikit-learn
How to read this roadmap

Lessons are ordered so that each concept is a prerequisite for the next. Badges tell you what to expect: Ready means the lesson is written and linked; From scratch means we build the algorithm in raw NumPy before touching Scikit-learn; Planned means it's on the path and I'll generate it when you reach it.

What you'll be able to do at the end

  • Explain what AI is, how the field developed, and precisely where ML sits inside it.
  • Describe the major approaches to building intelligent systems and why statistical learning came to dominate.
  • Reason about the ML core: models, parameters, loss functions, and optimization.
  • Prepare data, train models, evaluate them honestly, and interpret the results.
  • Implement Linear Regression, Logistic Regression, k-NN, Decision Trees, and k-Means from scratch.
  • Choose the right algorithm and evaluation strategy for a given problem.
  • Walk into your university AI/ML courses already fluent in the vocabulary and intuition.

Scope: deliberately stopping at Machine Learning

Per your goal, this path ends at classical ML. Deep Learning, neural networks, computer vision, NLP, reinforcement learning, and generative AI/LLMs are mentioned only to place ML in the wider landscape (Lessons 1, 3, and 29). Your mathematical foundations are handled separately at university — here, math appears only when it clarifies an idea.
